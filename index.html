02 / INSIDE THE DISCUSSIONS

Six AMCs.
Distinct perspectives.

Choose an AMC to see its sector preferences and investment approach. Views reflect the meeting record.

Read the full discussion by topic +
Global Uncertainties/ Other concerns
All the fund managers have shared their concerns on high crude oil price. According to them, crude prices are the real worry than geopolitical conflicts or wars. Weak monsoon is another threat (Tata and Kotak FMs highlighted this), due to this the rural demand will continue to face challenges. Continued FII selling remains an important factor to monitor. Weak performance in the IT sector has been a key factor affecting overall large cap fund performance. Growing concerns around data protection and cybersecurity remain an important business risk for many sectors.
FPI/DII activities/Q1 corporate earnings and view about Q2 earnings
Weak FII participation is expected to continue for 3rd quarter too. DIIs are supporting but it is limited. All fund managers believed that the Q1 earnings were either good or at least satisfactory. Few FMs think that the Q2 is expected to be reasonable, but Kotak and ICICI prudential fund managers are somewhat pessimistic on Q2.
Sectoral view in general
TRUST: Positive on industrials, financial services particularly with lending companies, consumer goods and health care. Negative on IT.
SBI: Positive on manufacturing, power, infrastructure, financial services but cautious with IT and Public sectors.
KOTAK: Positive on infrastructure, banking, manufacturing, and healthcare, while remaining cautious on IT, defence, insurance.
TATA: Positive on banking, manufacturing, pharmaceuticals, infrastructure, negative on IT
JM: Positive on manufacturing, export-oriented businesses, fintech. Cautious on banking and IT.
ICICI Pru: Positive on fertilisers, sugar, metals, textiles, automobiles, capital goods, and cement sectors while negative on IT.
View on lumpsum/staggered investments
ICICI has suggested to stagger the lumpsum investments for a period up to 6 months while all other fund managers have given green signal for lumpsum investments especially in large-cap funds. Trust and JM welcome lumpsum in all caps of funds.
View on prominent MF categories/which categories are better in the prevailing market condition
Almost all the FMs are positive on large caps, and they expect the long-term return from the segment to be around 12% to 13%, while 15% returns would be considered as a good outcome. ICICI is positive on mid-caps too whereas almost all the managers were of the view that small caps are still not in the safer zone to infuse money. Trust shared a different view; they are very positive on small caps.
A general view is that the SIFs can be considered for investors with appropriate risk appetite (as an alternative for small caps and micro caps).
View on HDFC Bank's recent developments
All fund managers had shared either neutral or negative view on HDFC Bank and are optimistic that the bank will come out from the ongoing internal challenges for sure, at the same time they are of the view that, unlike in the past, investors can expect only a relatively moderate growth in the coming years. Size of the bank has become huge, and it wouldn’t be easy for them to adopt new changes quickly.
Behavioural changes, if any, they noticed with the mindset of investors
Fund managers admitted that the style of mutual fund investments and the attitude of investors are rapidly changing.  AMFI’s “mutual fund sahi hai” campaign has been playing a key role in this. Investors are well informed and very enthusiastic to adapt to industry changes. AMCs are competing with each other to gain the mind share of investors not only in terms of fund performance but also in ease of investing.
Few other remarks made by FMs.
Among newly listed stocks, the manufacturing-focused companies will continue to provide investment opportunities.
FCNR deposits continue to be beneficial for banks.
China has made significant progress in the EV segment, the same trend will continue in India as well.

04 / TURN INSIGHTS INTO A DISCUSSION

Start with the client.
Then discuss the investment.

A suggested conversation flow for colleagues, based on the briefing.

ASK

“What is this money for,
and when will you need it?”

Understand the goal, time horizon, liquidity needs and comfort with volatility.

EXPLAIN

“Managers favour large caps,
but timing views differ.”

Present the shared view and the exceptions. Avoid turning opinions into return promises.

REVIEW

“Let’s review what fits
your existing portfolio.”

Use the approved fund list alongside the client’s risk profile, allocation and scheme information.
